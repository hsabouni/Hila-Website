Why I’m Using a Confederate in My Group Decision Study

A behind-the-scenes look at why my current study uses a trained research-team member to create a consistent teammate interaction, and what that choice makes possible to study.

A confederate is a member of the research team who participates in a study as though they were another participant. In this study, the confederate is also specifically trained to follow a consistent interaction protocol.

The study context

Participants first make subjective business decisions with a GenAI recommendation, then revisit those decisions after discussing them with a teammate.

To add: What decision task do participants complete, and what does the GenAI recommendation show them?

Why use a confederate?

Using a confederate helps isolate social influence from the many differences that naturally appear in a conversation. With participant pairs, one teammate may be more confident, persuasive, experienced, talkative, or personally invested than another. Those differences matter in real groups, but they can make it difficult to understand what caused a participant’s judgment to change.

Designing the teammate’s role

The confederate follows a defined social position while still participating in a conversation that needs to feel natural.

To add: What positions can the confederate take? What can they say, ask, or respond to, and what must remain consistent?

Consistency without sounding scripted

The methodological challenge is balancing experimental control with believable interaction. A rigid script may feel artificial, while unrestricted improvisation can make sessions difficult to compare.

To add: How is the confederate trained? Which parts are scripted, and where can they respond naturally?

What I am measuring

The design allows me to examine how a teammate’s position may relate to changes in a participant’s opinion, confidence, verification needs, and final decision when using GenAI.

What this design is teaching me

Another person’s judgment is not simply background noise in group decision-making. It can become part of how people interpret GenAI, assess their own position, and decide what to do next.
