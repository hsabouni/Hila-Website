Designing a Confederate for a Live Study

Once I decided to use a confederate in my group decision study, the method depended on more than writing a set of lines. I had to design the script and the person delivering it together. The script needed to hold the experimental condition steady, while the confederate needed to make the exchange feel like a natural interaction between two participants.

Designing neutrality for this study

Writing the script was more difficult than simply producing a neutral response. Neutrality can mean different things across contexts and populations. A response that seems balanced in one setting may sound distant or persuasive in another.

I combined a review of the literature with ten pilot sessions in which I tried different response lengths and wording. Each version helped reveal where a response felt too brief, too elaborate, or less natural for the interaction. The goal was not to create a universally neutral script. It was to develop neutral responses that fit this study and remained aligned with what the experiment needed to test.

Delivering the protocol live

The confederate was not simply reciting a script. Because the interaction unfolded live, she had to listen carefully to each participant, identify their response, and select the appropriate scripted reply in real time. She needed to do this quickly and accurately without interrupting the flow of the conversation or revealing her knowledge of the protocol.

This required practice as well as familiarity with every path the conversation could take. The confederate needed to understand the study well enough to make the correct protocol decision in the moment, while still appearing like a participant encountering the task for the first time.

Naturalness is part of control

Because the study examines the effect of an expressed position, the delivery of that position also matters. The confederate practiced keeping her tone, body language, facial expression, timing, and other reactions as consistent as possible across sessions. The script needed to remain neutral; the person delivering it needed to remain natural.

Those goals can pull in different directions. Delivery that is too controlled can feel mechanical, while delivery that is too expressive can introduce variation the study was not designed to test. Piloting helped calibrate that balance so the interaction could feel ordinary while the protocol remained precise.

Designing the role as part of the method

The confederate is a methodological tool, but designing her role became part of the research itself. Every decision about what she says, how she says it, and what she deliberately leaves out reflects a larger challenge: studying social influence without removing the social qualities that make the interaction believable.
