How People Make Subjective Decisions with AI Under Social Influence

PhD Research · Human–AI Interaction · Ongoing

I study how people evaluate generative AI recommendations when decisions are subjective, consequential, and made with others. Rather than treating AI use as an interaction between one person and one system, this research examines what happens when AI recommendations enter group discussion—where people interpret the technology, evaluate one another’s judgments, and negotiate what to trust before reaching a decision.

My role Lead researcher, end to end

I lead the program from question formulation and study design through data collection, analysis, interpretation, and communication.

Research context One program developed across two phases

The foundational study examined disciplinary differences in AI verification. Its findings led to the main study on how a teammate’s agreement or disagreement changes evaluation of AI recommendations.

Evidence so far One finding established; the main test is underway

Phase 1: Engineers reported stronger expectations that AI outputs should be verified, despite similar use and perceived value across disciplines. Phase 2: Data collection and analysis are examining how a teammate’s stance changes decisions, confidence, and information seeking.

How the Research Evolved

01

Foundational study

Disciplinary Differences in AI Verification

Do engineers and designers approach the verification of AI-generated outputs differently?

A mixed-method survey of 117 engineering and design students examined AI use, confidence, perceived value, trust, and expectations for verification in individual and team settings.

What it established: Both groups used and valued AI similarly, but engineers reported stronger expectations that AI outputs should be verified. Disciplinary training may shape standards of evaluation more than adoption itself.

The question this opened

If people bring different verification expectations into collaboration, can peer pressure determine whose judgment shapes the group’s response to AI?

02

Main study · Current phase

Social Influence in AI-Assisted Decision-Making

How does a teammate’s agreement or disagreement affect decision-making with an AI recommendation?

In a controlled within-subject experiment, graduate students first make subjective business decisions with ChatGPT recommendations independently, then revisit those decisions after discussing them with a teammate following a standardized agreement or disagreement script.

What it measures: Changes in decisions, confidence, and intention to seek additional information. The design isolates the social effect of a teammate’s stance without adding new evidence to the discussion.

Data collection and analysis are ongoing. Findings will be added when the evidence is ready.

Design Opportunity

This work can inform collaborative AI systems that protect independent judgment while supporting productive deliberation.

Possible interventions include collecting private evaluations before group discussion, making uncertainty visible without turning it into pressure, and prompting teams to distinguish social agreement from evidence-based verification.
